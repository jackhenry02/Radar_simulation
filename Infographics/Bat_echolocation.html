Core Task: Range Estimation

We compare the proposed model's range-finding mechanisms against established biologically-plausible models. State-of-the-art (SoA) models often replicate "Delay-Tuned Neurons" (DTNs) found in the bat's Inferior Colliculus (IC), which are highly plausible but can be complex to train. The proposed supervised SNN shows high performance, though with less direct biological mapping.

Capability Gaps: Velocity & Localization

While strong in range estimation, the current "Spike-FMCW" model lacks mechanisms for critical bat abilities. These include Doppler Shift Compensation (DSC) for velocity and processing binaural cues (ITD/ILD) for 3D localization. Existing SoA research provides clear neuro-inspired models for these tasks.

How Do Bat-Models Learn?

A survey of SNN echolocation models shows a clear trend. Spike-Timing-Dependent Plasticity (STDP) is a favored mechanism for its biological plausibility in learning echo delays. However, surrogate gradients (as used in the proposed model) are increasingly popular for their high performance in engineering applications.

Originality & Future Work

The primary novelty of the "Spike-FMCW" model is its end-to-end spiking architecture. Most research assumes a classical signal and models only the processor. The SNN-based transmitter is a significant contribution.
